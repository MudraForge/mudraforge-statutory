# ICAI Guidance: Accounting for Employee Share-Based Payments (Ind AS 102 / IFRS 2 / ASC 718)

## 1. Scope & Core Principle
Ind AS 102 specifies the financial reporting by an entity when it undertakes a share-based payment transaction. For equity-settled transactions, goods or services received are measured at the fair value of equity instruments granted at grant date, credited to Share-Based Payment Reserve.

## 2. Graded Vesting vs Straight-Line Amortization
Under Ind AS 102, paragraph 15, if stock options vest in installments over the vesting period (e.g., 25% after 1 year, 25% after 2 years, 25% after 3 years, 25% after 4 years):
- **Mandatory Separate Tranche Treatment**: Each installment (tranche) must be accounted for as a separate award with its own service period.
- **Front-Loaded P&L Charge**:
  - Tranche 1 (25%) is expensed over 1 year (100% in Year 1).
  - Tranche 2 (25%) is expensed over 2 years (50% Year 1, 50% Year 2).
  - Tranche 3 (25%) is expensed over 3 years (33.3% Year 1, 33.3% Year 2, 33.3% Year 3).
  - Tranche 4 (25%) is expensed over 4 years (25% each across Years 1-4).
- **Year 1 Total Expense**: 25% + 12.5% + 8.33% + 6.25% = 52.08% of the total grant-date fair value is recognized in Year 1 alone. Single straight-line averaging (25% per year) is strictly prohibited under Ind AS 102.

## 3. Group / Cross-Border Recharge Agreements
Where a parent entity (e.g., Delaware C-Corp) grants options over its own shares to employees of an Indian subsidiary:
- If the subsidiary has no obligation to settle, the subsidiary accounts for the transaction as **equity-settled**, recognizing employee compensation expense and a capital contribution from parent.
- If an intercompany recharge agreement mandates the subsidiary to reimburse the parent in cash upon exercise:
  - The recharge obligation is accounted for as a distribution of equity or intercompany liability up to the fair value recognized.

## 4. Indian Income Tax Deduction (Biocon Special Bench Principle)
Pursuant to the landmark Special Bench ruling in *CIT v. Biocon Ltd* (affirmed by the Supreme Court of India):
- The discount on issue of ESOPs (FMV at grant minus exercise price) is not a notional loss, but an **ascertained liability incurred for the purpose of business**.
- It is allowable as a deductible business expenditure under **Section 37(1)** of the Income-tax Act, 1961, spread over the vesting period.
