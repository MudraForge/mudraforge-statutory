# MCA Notification: Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015 — Ind AS 103 Business Combinations

## 1. Executive Summary & Statutory Background
Ind AS 103 establishes principles and requirements for how an acquirer:
- Recognizes and measures in its financial statements the identifiable assets acquired, the liabilities assumed, and any non-controlling interest in the acquiree.
- Recognizes and measures the goodwill acquired in the business combination or a gain from a bargain purchase.
- Determines what information to disclose to enable users of the financial statements to evaluate the nature and financial effects of the business combination.

Ind AS 103 is converged with International Financial Reporting Standard 3 (IFRS 3) and conceptually aligned with US GAAP ASC Topic 805.

## 2. The Four Pillars of the Acquisition Method
Every business combination within the scope of Ind AS 103 must be accounted for using the **acquisition method**:
1. **Identifying the Acquirer**: The entity that obtains control of another entity (the acquiree) per Ind AS 110 principles.
2. **Determining the Acquisition Date**: The date on which the acquirer obtains control of the acquiree (generally the closing date when legal transfer occurs and consideration is paid).
3. **Recognizing and Measuring Identifiable Assets, Liabilities, and NCI**:
   - Assets and liabilities are measured at their acquisition-date fair values.
   - Intangible assets meeting either the contractual-legal criterion or the separability criterion (e.g. brand names, customer contracts, proprietary source code) must be recognized separately from goodwill.
   - Corresponding deferred tax liabilities (DTLs) are recognized on the step-up of asset book values over tax bases under Ind AS 12.
4. **Recognizing and Measuring Goodwill or Bargain Purchase Gain**:
   - `Goodwill = Consideration Transferred + Non-Controlling Interest + Pre-existing Interest - Net Identifiable Assets`.
   - Goodwill is capitalized and tested for impairment at least annually under Ind AS 36.

## 3. Treatment of Contingent Consideration (Earnouts)
- Contingent consideration must be recognized at fair value on the acquisition date as part of the consideration transferred.
- If classified as equity, it is not remeasured and settlement is accounted for within equity.
- If classified as a financial liability, subsequent changes in fair value are recognized in Profit or Loss under Ind AS 109.

## 4. Prohibition on Capitalizing Transaction Costs
All acquisition-related costs (finder's fees, legal, accounting, valuation, advisory fees) must be expensed in the periods in which they are incurred. They cannot be included in purchase consideration or capitalized into goodwill.
