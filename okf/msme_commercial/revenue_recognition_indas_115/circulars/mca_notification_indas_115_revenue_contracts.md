# Ministry of Corporate Affairs — Notification G.S.R. 310(E)

## Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Amendment Rules, 2018: Ind AS 115

**Authority**: Ministry of Corporate Affairs, New Delhi  
**Effective Date**: 1st April, 2018  
**Applicability**: Mandatory for all companies governed by Ind AS under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013  

### Statutory Objective
The Central Government notified the **Indian Accounting Standard (Ind AS) 115: Revenue from Contracts with Customers**, converging verbatim with **IFRS 15** and aligning with **US GAAP ASC Topic 606**. Ind AS 115 supersedes the prior standards **Ind AS 18 (Revenue)** and **Ind AS 11 (Construction Contracts)**, establishing a single comprehensive revenue recognition framework across all industries.

### Core Provisions of the 5-Step Model

1. **Step 1: Identify the Contract with a Customer**
   - The contract must have commercial substance, approval and commitment from all parties, identifiable rights regarding goods/services, identifiable payment terms, and probable collectibility of consideration.

2. **Step 2: Identify the Performance Obligations in the Contract**
   - At contract inception, assess promised goods or services to determine if they are distinct.
   - A good or service is distinct if:
     a) The customer can benefit from the good or service either on its own or together with other resources readily available (*capable of being distinct*).
     b) The promise to transfer the good or service is separately identifiable from other promises in the contract (*distinct within the context of the contract*).

3. **Step 3: Determine the Transaction Price**
   - The transaction price is the consideration expected to be entitled to, excluding third-party collections (e.g. GST).
   - Variable consideration (rebates, discounts, refunds, milestone incentives, performance penalties) must be estimated using either the expected value or most likely amount method, subject to the constraint that it is highly probable that a significant reversal of revenue will not occur.

4. **Step 4: Allocate the Transaction Price to Performance Obligations**
   - The transaction price must be allocated to each distinct performance obligation in proportion to its relative **Standalone Selling Price (SSP)** determined at contract inception.
   - Observable prices take precedence over estimated methods (adjusted market assessment, expected cost plus margin, or residual approach).

5. **Step 5: Recognize Revenue When or As Performance Obligations Are Satisfied**
   - Revenue is recognized when control transfers to the customer:
     - **Over Time**: If (i) customer simultaneously receives and consumes benefits, (ii) performance creates or enhances an asset controlled by customer, or (iii) performance does not create an asset with alternative use and entity has an enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date.
     - **At a Point in Time**: If none of the over-time criteria are met, control transfers at a specific point in time (based on physical possession, legal title, risks and rewards, or customer acceptance).

### Balance Sheet Classification: Contract Assets vs. Liabilities
- **Contract Asset**: An entity's right to consideration in exchange for goods or services transferred to the customer when that right is conditioned on something other than the passage of time (e.g., unbilled revenue prior to milestone billing).
- **Contract Liability**: An entity's obligation to transfer goods or services to a customer for which the entity has received consideration (or an amount of consideration is due) from the customer (e.g., deferred revenue, customer advances).

### Cross-Border & Dual-Reporting Synchronization
Entities operating cross-border structures (e.g. US Parent with Indian Operating Subsidiary) must establish unified contract performance schedules ensuring that contract asset and liability balances synchronize across:
- **Ind AS 115** (Indian Statutory Reporting)
- **US GAAP ASC 606** (US SEC & Parent Consolidation)
- **ICDS IV** (Income Computation and Disclosure Standard IV for Indian Income Tax filing)
